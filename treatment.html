Disease and Symptoms

Disease

Polio infects the throat and intestines. It is spread through coughing, sneezing or coming in contact with feces of an infected person and then ingesting the germs. It can also be spread through contaminated water. Polio is highly contagious. Polio has three variations, wild poliovirus, type 1, 2 and 3. Wild polio types 2 and 3 have been eradicated and wild polio type 1 only exists in certian areas, it is also the most likely to cause paralysis. The majority of people with non-paralytic polio survive. Children are the most affected and at-risk population for polio, more on that in treatments and history.

Symptoms

Fatigue, fever, headache, vomiting, constipation, diharrea, sore throat, neck stiffness, sensitivity to light (photophobia), muscle pain, weakness or paralysis, and difficulty breathing, swallowing or talking are all symptoms of poliovirus. Up to 95% of people who get polio do not experience symptoms.

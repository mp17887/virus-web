Types of Life Cycles

Lytic

Lytic infections are fast working and cause the host cell to lyse. There are 5 steps to the process, attachment, entry, replication, assembly and release. Attachment is when the virus binds to the host cell, and entry is when the genetic material is injected into the cell. Replication is when the virus immediately takes control of the host's cell metabolism, this leads to the cell's organelles producing new proteins and nucleic acids.
Assembly is when nucleic acids and proteins join to form new viruses, finally, release is when viral enzymes make the cell burst, this releases viruses into the rest of the environment. The viruses that are released are capable of infecting other cells.

Lysogenic

Lysogenic infections do not kill the host cell and they take longer to have an effect. Attachment and entry are the same as in lytic infections. The difference is that once the genetic material enters the cell it is not immediately copied to make proteins, instead it recombines with a specific region of the host cells' chromosome. This causes the virus genetic material to be integrated into the chromosome, this is called a prophage and is not active.
This causes each cell that is created from cell division to have the virus' genetic material. The prophage becomes active when the cell reaches the correct conditions and continues the lytic cycle, genetic material copying and protein synthesis, phage assembly and lysis.

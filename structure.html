Parts and Structures

Basic Virus Structure

The structure of viruses is extremely simple, with the two main things being genetic material, DNA or RNA, and a capsid which is a protective coat made of protein. Bacteriophages, for example, are more complex in their structure, more on that below.

Classification

DNA viruses use DNA as their genetic material.
RNA viruses use RNA as their genetic material, they can also be referred to as a retrovirus.

4 Types of Viruses

Helical Virus

Made of a single type of capsomeres stacked around a central axis to form a helical structure. Helix can have a hollow center, making it look like a hollow tube

Icosahedral Virus

Equilateral triangular capsomeres fuse to form a sphere.

Complex Virus

Have a capsid that is not purely helical or icosahedral. They often have extra structures like protein tails or an outer wall. Phage viruses are a good example of extra structures, they have an icosahedral head with the genetic material inside and a helical tail.

Enveloped Virus

These viruses surround themselves in a portion of their host cells' cell membrane and it therefore gains a phospho lipid bilayer, aka, viral envelope. This allows the cell to trick other cells in the organism and make it easier to infiltrate and take over. This type of virus is the most evolved.
